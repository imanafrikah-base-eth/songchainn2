The SONGCHAINN Standard

Every track in the catalog, measured to ITU-R BS.1770-4, to answer one question: what is the production standard that the app already holds, expressed as numbers instead of one person’s ear?

The finding

Your catalog is not one standard. It is two, and only 56 of the 230 tracks are the standard you think you are protecting.

The lossless half is disciplined and consistent: it never once passes −2.4 dBTP. The other half runs roughly 5 LU louder, was rendered from lossy sources, and clips. Calibrating a gate on the catalog as a whole would average those two together and produce a threshold that protects neither.

Loudness follows ITU-R BS.1770-4 with K-weighting derived per sample rate and gated 400 ms blocks. Before it touched your music the implementation was checked against the EBU Tech 3341 conformance case, reading −22.99 LUFS where the standard requires −23.0 ±0.1, and its filter coefficients match the values tabulated in the spec to nine decimal places. True peak uses 4× oversampling and resolves the textbook intersample case to within 0.11 dB. Twenty-six checks in total, all passing.

A correction worth stating

My first pass reported that 89% of the catalog was band-limited and 40% was “lossy in a WAV wrapper.” That was wrong, and I threw the number out. Energy rolloff cannot detect lossy encoding, because treble carries a tiny share of total energy, so a 99.9%-energy rolloff sits near 14 kHz on any healthy master. The figures here come from a different test: a genuine brick wall, meaning the spectrum falls off a cliff at a fixed frequency and stays at the floor above it. A clean 7ROO7H BASED master reads a 22,043 Hz cutoff with a 0.1 dB slope. A flagged one reads 20,180 Hz with a 54 dB cliff. That separation is not ambiguous.

The clean rows matter as much as the bad ones. Nothing is squashed, nothing has DC offset, nothing is channel-imbalanced. The engineering discipline is real. What went wrong went wrong at delivery, not at the mix.

The worst offenders are concentrated rather than spread. NDA’s married to the grind reaches +3.6 dBTP with 1,719 sustained clipped runs; ELEMENT reaches +3.38 dBTP with 2,238. The decision this forces

Applied honestly, this gate rejects most of your existing catalog. 129 tracks fail on true peak alone and 173 fail the lossy-source test. That is not a reason to soften the numbers, it is the actual finding: the bar you are protecting in conversation is higher than the bar the library currently meets. You have three options, and it is your call, not the analyser’s. Grandfather the existing catalog and hold new uploads to the standard. Or re-master and re-deliver the affected tracks from source, which is real work but permanently fixes the shelf. Or set the launch gate at reject-above-0 dBTP only, which most of the catalog does pass, and tighten later.

Schema

The static catalog has artist ids; the database does not. Any grouping done from the database has only the name string to work with, which is exactly how the spelling split became invisible. Worth its own cleanup.

Method Integrated loudness, loudness range and gating per ITU-R BS.1770-4 and EBU R128, K-weighting coefficients derived at each file’s native sample rate rather than resampling.
